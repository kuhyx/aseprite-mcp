## 0. The headline finding (answers Q1 + Q2 together)

Your suspicion (a) is correct, but the mechanism is more specific than "my
circles aren't round". **The circles are the right width. Their run-lengths
progress in a stuttering sequence rather than a smooth one**, and that stutter
is what the eye reads as "squashed".

The diagnostic is not the row lengths themselves — it's the **deltas between
consecutive row lengths** as you move from the top of the circle toward the
equator.

- A circle that reads as round has deltas that **decrease monotonically**:
  `4,2,2,0,0` or `2,2,2,0,0`. Big steps at the poles, tapering to zero at the
  equator. Never a step that grows again after shrinking.
- A circle that reads as squashed/blobby has a **stutter**: `4,0,2,0` or
  `4,2,0,2,0`. A delta of 0 followed by a delta of 2 is the defect. It puts a
  flat spot in the middle of the arc and then a corner right after it.

This matches the Lospec rule for curves in prose: put the longest segments at
the N/S/E/W cardinals and let them shrink smoothly toward the diagonals; a
segment length that jumps around "gives the line a little lump in the middle".

**IMPORTANT CAVEAT — added after measuring a shipped sprite that contradicts
the strong form of this rule.** Kyrise's `pearl_01a.png`, a professional 16x16
round object, has run-lengths **byte-identical to Aseprite's d=14 ellipse**,
stutter included (`deltas 4,2,2,0,2`) — and it reads as convincingly round.
See §2a. So:

- The delta rule is a **tie-breaker, not a pass/fail gate**. Prefer smoother
  deltas when choosing between candidates; do not reject a silhouette solely
  because it stutters.
- At d=14+ the silhouette alone does not decide round vs squashed. **Diameter
  and shading dominate** — see §2a point 3.
- The one diameter with a confirmed problem and no shipped counterexample is
  **Aseprite's d=12** (4px pole, deltas `4,2,0,2`). That warning stands. See §1.

### 1b. Better patterns from a distance threshold `[ALGORITHMIC]`

There is **no single canonical "pixel circle chart"**. I looked; the top
results are Minecraft block-circle generators and Pinterest boards, not an
authority. So rather than invent one, I generated circles with a stated
algorithm and picked by the delta rule from §0.

Algorithm — pixel-center distance test, with a tunable radius bias `eps`:

```python
def rows_threshold(d, eps=0.0):
    r = d / 2.0
    out = []
    for y in range(d):
        cy = y + 0.5 - r
        n = sum(1 for x in range(d)
                if (x + 0.5 - r) ** 2 + cy * cy <= (r + eps) ** 2)
        out.append(n)
    return out
```

`eps` biases the threshold outward. `eps=0` is the textbook test and, notably,
reproduces Aseprite's output almost exactly — including its stutters. **`eps`
between 0.25 and 0.5 is where the good circles live.** That is the single most
useful number in this document.

deltas):**

## 2. Q2 — Why small circles read as "squashed"

Ranked by how much damage each does at 16x16, all derived from the delta rule:

1. **Stutter in the delta sequence** (`...,0,2,...`). The dominant cause. A row
   that doesn't grow, followed by a row that grows by 2, is a visible dent
   plus a corner. See the measured Aseprite d=12/14/16 above.
2. **A pole flat that is too short relative to the diameter.** A 4px cap on a
   12px circle reads pinched — the curvature has to be crammed into too few
   rows, forcing large deltas. Rule of thumb from the monotonic set above: the
   pole run should be roughly `d/2` rounded to even, i.e. 6 for d=12, 8 for
   d=14/16, 6 for d=10.
3. **Too many rows at full width.** If 6 of 12 rows are all `12` wide, the
   silhouette is a rounded rectangle, not a circle. The equator should hold for
   about `d/3` rows, not `d/2`.
4. **Asymmetry between the horizontal and vertical profile.** Verify by
   transposing: count *column* runs and check they match the row runs. A circle
   whose columns differ from its rows is literally an ellipse.
5. **The outline tax (see §8).** Drawing a d=12 circle and then adding a 1px
   dark outline *outside* it makes a d=14 object; adding it *inside* leaves a
   d=10 interior. Either way the fill is no longer the size you designed.

**The rule for run-length progression, stated as a check you can run:**
take the row lengths, take the first differences over the top half, and assert
that sequence is non-increasing and ends in zeros. `2,2,2,0,0` passes.
`4,2,0,2` fails. This is mechanical — you can assert it in code rather than
eyeballing it.

**What this actually means — and it changes the advice:**

1. **At d=14, silhouette arithmetic alone does not discriminate round from
   squashed.** The delta rule is a useful *lens*, not a pass/fail gate. Demote
   it to a tie-breaker: when two candidate silhouettes are available, prefer
   the smoother deltas; do not reject a silhouette solely because it stutters.
2. **The d=12 warning still stands, with that narrower scope.** Aseprite's
   d=12 (`4,8,10,10,12,...`, deltas `4,2,0,2`) has **no shipped counterexample**
   in anything I measured, and its 4px pole on a 12px circle is genuinely
   blunt. Avoid that one. I can no longer tell you to avoid d=14 or d=16.
3. **So what makes the pearl work, if not the silhouette?** Three things the
   stutter debate was distracting from:
   - **Size.** d=14 filling rows 1..14 of a 16-row canvas. It is nearly the
     entire icon.
   - **An offset highlight arc** in the upper-left (rows 4-7, the `-`/`o`
     cluster at x≈4-7), not a centred or symmetric one.
   - **Four distinct tone steps** (`#`, `+`, `o`, `-`), not two.

**This reframes the likely root cause of your bomb / potion / coin failures
away from silhouette arithmetic and toward diameter and shading.** Before
re-deriving run-lengths, check the cheaper things first: is your round object
actually ~14px across, and does it have an offset highlight plus 3-4 tones? A
d=10 circle with flat fill will read as squashed no matter how perfect its
deltas are.

## 3. Q3 — Gem bottoms and shield bottoms

**Your suspicion (b) is confirmed, and it is the single easiest fix in this
document.** Measured across every gem, crystal and shield in a shipped
professional 16x16 pack: **nothing terminates in a 1px point.**

### 3a. Gem `[MEASURED — Kyrise gem_01a.png, gem_01b.png]`

```

 2 |.....#o-+#......| 5
 3 |....#o--o+#.....| 7
 4 |...#o---oo+#....| 9
 5 |..#o----ooo+#...| 11
 6 |.#o------ooo+#..| 13    <- girdle (widest)
 7 |.#-----------#..| 13    <- girdle held for 2 rows
 8 |.##+++oo+#####..| 13
 9 |..##+++o#####...| 11
10 |...##++o####....| 9
11 |....##+o###.....| 7
12 |.....##o##......| 5
13 |......###.......| 3     <- pavilion: 3px FLAT, not a point
runs  3,5,7,9,11,13,13,13,11,9,7,5,3
```

**The pavilion ends in a 3-pixel flat run, not a 1px point.** The taper is a
perfectly regular `-2` per row all the way down — `13,11,9,7,5,3` — and then it
simply stops. It never reaches 1.

This is exactly your "weird triangle": a shape that tapers `...5,3,1` has a
single lonely pixel hanging off the bottom, which the eye reads as a spike or a
dropped pixel rather than as a facet. **Truncate at 3.** If you need it to feel
sharper, truncate at 2, but never at 1.

The one exception measured — `crystal_01b.png` — *does* go to 1px:

```
11 |....#+o-o+#.....| 7
12 |.....#+-+#......| 5
13 |......#o#.......| 3
14 |.......#........| 1    <- 1px tip IS used here
runs  5,9,11,13,15,15,15,13,11,9,7,5,3,1
```

But note this is a *crystal shard*, an explicitly pointed object, and the 1px
tip is the last of a long regular `-2` taper on a 15px-wide body. A 1px
terminus works when it is the endpoint of a long, consistent taper. It fails
when it terminates a short one — which is what a 16x16 gem or shield has room
for.

**Why yours reads as a "weird triangle":** a shield that goes `13,9,5,1` is
tapering at `-4` per row and reaching a point. That is a triangle, literally —
a 45-degree wedge. A heater shield's bottom is a *curve* that happens to be
narrower at the bottom. The fix is the same as the gem's: taper at **-2 per
row**, over **4+ rows**, and **stop at 5** (shield) or **3** (gem).

**The general rule for both, and the direct answer to your suspicion (b):**

> Never taper a silhouette to 1px at the bottom of a 16x16 icon unless the
> taper is at least 6 rows long and runs at a constant -2. Terminate wide:
> 3px for a gem, 5px for a shield. The last row you draw should still look
> like a *run*, not a *pixel*.

**The four things that make it a sword and not a dagger:**

1. **Blade occupies ~11 of 16 rows — about 65-70% of total height.** If your
   blade is half the icon or less, you have drawn a dagger. That is the
   definition of the silhouette difference.
2. **The blade is drawn on the DIAGONAL, corner to corner.** Every single
   shipped sword does this. It is not a style choice — it is arithmetic. A
   vertical blade at 16x16 can be at most 16px long. A diagonal blade across a
   16x16 box is `16*sqrt(2) ≈ 22px` long. **The diagonal buys you 40% more
   blade for free.** This is the highest-leverage fix available for the sword.
3. **The grip is SHORT — 3 to 4 rows, and narrow (2-3px).** A dagger reads as a
   dagger partly because its hilt is large *relative to* its blade. Shrink the
   hilt and the same blade suddenly reads as longer.
4. **The tip is 2-4px wide, not 1px.** Same rule as the gem (§3): these blades
   taper to a 2-4px run at row 0, never a single pixel. The point is implied by
   the diagonal edge, not by an actual 1px apex.

**Blade-to-hilt ratio, stated as the number you asked for:**

> **blade : guard : grip+pommel = 11 : 1 : 4** of 16 rows.
> Measured mean across 3 shipped sprites. Blade ≈ 0.69 of total height.
> A dagger inverts toward roughly 8 : 1 : 7.

## 5. Q5 — Coin face, and why yours reads as the Eye of Sauron

`[MEASURED — Kyrise coin_01a.png]`

```
 4 |.....#####......| 5
 5 |....#ooooo#.....| 7
 6 |...#o+++o+o#....| 9
 7 |...#o++o++o#....| 9
 8 |...##+o+++##....| 9
 9 |....#######.....| 7
10 |.....#####......| 5
runs 5,7,9,9,9,7,5   -- d=9, deltas 2,2,0
```

**The face is blank.** There is no centred symbol, no star, no currency mark.
The interior is pure shading: a light rim on the upper-left, mid-tone body,
dark edge on the lower-right. The pixels that aren't the outline are a
*gradient*, not a *device*.

**Why a centred mark reads as an eye:** a circle with a distinct, contrasting,
concentric shape at its exact centre is the definition of an iris-and-pupil.
The eye is one of the most over-trained shapes in human vision — anything
matching `[round outline] + [contrasting round-ish blob at centre]` will be
read as an eye first and as anything else second. Adding a *rim* around it
(which a coin has) makes it worse: rim + iris + pupil is three concentric
rings, which is precisely the Eye of Sauron.

**Four ways to signal "coin" without a face symbol, ranked:**

1. **Leave the face blank; carry it entirely on shading.** What the shipped
   sprite does. Light source upper-left: bright arc top-left, mid body,
   dark arc bottom-right. This reads as a *disc*, and a metallic disc at
   16x16 in an RPG is a coin.
2. **Stack two or three coins.** `[MEASURED — coin_04b.png]` does exactly this
   — two d=9 discs offset diagonally, bbox 14x13:
   ```
    2 |...#####........| 5
    3 |..#-----#.......| 7
    4 |.#-ooo-o-#......| 9
    ...
    8 |.#+o-ooo#####...| 12   <- second coin starts, overlapping
    9 |.#o+o++#-----#..| 13
   10 |.#+o-o#-ooo-o-#.| 14
   ...
   14 |........#####...| 5
   ```
   A stack is unambiguous — no single disc can be mistaken for an eye, a
   button, or a ball. **This is the strongest fix for your coin.**
3. **Break the symmetry.** Put the highlight off-centre and make the rim
   thicker on one side. An eye is bilaterally symmetric; a lit disc is not.
4. **If you must have a mark, make it OFF-CENTRE or non-round.** A tiny
   off-centre star, or a stamped letter, at 2-3px. Never a filled circle at
   the exact centre.

**Every single one is wider than it is tall.** A tongue is *taller than wide*,
symmetric about a vertical axis, and tapers to a rounded tip at the bottom. If
your meat is a vertical oval with a rounded bottom, you have drawn a tongue —

Structure: a **small bone knob at one end** (rows 9-15, only 3-4px wide),
then the meat mass swelling **diagonally away** from it and tapering to a

### 6b. Structural cues that make meat read as meat, in priority order

1. **Land it wide, not tall.** Aspect ratio at least 1.3:1 horizontal. This
   alone kills the tongue read.
2. **Add a bone.** It is the single most legible "this is meat" signal
   available, and it costs 3-5 pixels. Two options, both measured:
   - **Hock/drumstick**: a 2-3px light stub protruding from ONE end, at an
     angle, distinct in colour (near-white) from the flesh.
   - **T-bone**: the bone runs *through* the mass. In the bluecarrot sprite
     the bone is drawn as a light `-`/`o` vertical-ish line at x≈12-14 rows
     6-17, splitting the steak into a large lobe and a small lobe.
3. **Break the outline's symmetry.** All three sprites have lumpy, irregular
   silhouettes — no smooth ovals. A tongue is smooth; a cut of meat is not.
4. **Two-tone the interior: dark red-brown flesh + lighter fat.** The
   7soul1 roast puts the light tone as a *rind along one edge* (see the `o`
   band down the right side, rows 12-20) — that reads as fat cap and is very
   legible. Marbling as scattered single pixels is secondary; the fat *rind*
   along an edge does more work.
5. **Do not make it pink and smooth.** Pink + smooth + vertical + tapered is
   the tongue. Brown/maroon + lumpy + horizontal + bone is meat.

## 7. Q7 — Ring

`[MEASURED — Kyrise ring_01a.png, ring_02a.png, ring_03a.png — all three
identical in the band, differing only in the gem on top]`

```
 2 |.....######.....| 6
 3 |....#++++++#....| 8
 4 |...##++++++##...| 10
 5 |...##++++++##...| 10
 6 |...#++####++#...| 10   <- hole opens
 7 |...#+#....#+#...| 6    <- band is 2px each side, hole is 4px
 8 |...#+#....#+#...| 6
 9 |...#+#....#+#...| 6
10 |...#++####++#...| 10   <- hole closes
11 |...##########...| 10
12 |....##++++##....| 8
13 |.....######.....| 6
```

**The numbers you asked for:**

| quantity | measured value |
|---|---|
| outer bbox | **10 wide x 12 tall** |
| band thickness | **2px** (1px dark outline + 1px fill) |
| hole (inner) | **4 wide x 4 tall** |
| rows where hole is open | 4 of 12 |

**How they made a 10px annulus read as round rather than square:**

1. **The outer silhouette follows the smooth-delta rule.** Outer runs are
   `6,8,10,10,10,10,10,10,10,10,8,6` — deltas `2,2,0,0,0`. Textbook monotonic
   (compare §1b's recommended d=10, which is `6,8,10,10,10,...` — the same
   sequence). The roundness lives entirely in the outer edge.
2. **The hole is NOT round — it is a 4x4 square.** This is the surprising and
   important part. At this size there is no room to make the inner edge round,
   so they didn't try. A 4x4 square hole inside a round outer silhouette reads
   as a ring. Trying to round a 4px hole produces a 4px blob with chewed
   corners, which reads as noise.
3. **The band is 2px, and 2px is the minimum.** At 1px the band is a hairline
   that disappears at a distance and looks like an outline with nothing inside.
   At 3px on a 10px ring the hole drops to 2px and vanishes.

**Minimum diameter for a convincing ring band — arithmetic, not opinion:**

```
outer d  -  2 x band(2px)  =  hole
   8     -        4        =   4    workable but cramped
  10     -        4        =   6    (measured sprites use 10 outer, 4 hole,
                                     because the band thickens at the sides)
  12     -        4        =   8    comfortable
```

> **Minimum outer diameter for a ring at 16x16 is 10px with a 2px band.**
> Below d=10 the hole is too small to read as a hole. Do not go to a 1px band
> to buy a bigger hole — that trades a readable ring for a readable circle.

Note the shipped rings are **10 wide x 12 tall** — slightly *taller* than wide,
because rows 10-11 are a solid band (the bottom of the ring is thicker, as if
seen at a slight angle). That extra solidity at the bottom is what stops it
looking like a flat washer.

second reading is more likely. **Add the cork + pinched neck before chasing

## 8. The outline tax — applies to every icon above

A constraint worth stating explicitly because it silently breaks all the
numbers above. These icons carry a 1px dark outline. So:

- An object with a **d=14 outer silhouette** has a **d=12 interior** to shade.
- An object with a **d=10 outer silhouette** (your ring) has a **d=8 interior**.

If you design the fill at d=12 and then add an outline outside it, you have
built a d=14 object and it may no longer fit its 16x16 box with a margin. If
you add the outline *inside*, your carefully-chosen d=12 run-lengths are now
describing the outline, and the fill is an uncontrolled d=10.

**Decide which one the run-length table describes, and be consistent.** The
tables in §1 and all measured sprites above describe the **outer silhouette,
outline included**. The pearl's `4,8,10,12,12,14,...` counts the dark outline
pixels as part of the run.

**A likely contributing cause of "squashed":** if the fill was sized as though
the outline were free, the visible coloured area is 2px smaller in each
dimension than intended, and the outline's own run-lengths were never checked
against the delta rule at all.
